# Learning from their posts

Their past posts show how they really write and choose. Start with what needs nothing installed.

## Ways in, simplest first

1. Screenshots: in the chat, or saved by them into `memory/sources/`. Read captions, emoji, hashtags, how many photos.
2. Their platform's data export, placed in `memory/sources/` and read there. Own posts come with images and captions.
   Likes and saved posts are often only links. Say which ones you could not see and ask for screenshots of a few
   instead of guessing what they were.
3. Public posts through an official open API, such as Bluesky's. Public accounts only.
4. If your app has a browser tool: read only the accounts they are logged into and told you to read, at a human pace
   (a page at a time, with pauses), and stop the moment they ask.

## What to do with it

Read for habits: caption length, languages, casing, emoji, tags, how many photos a post has, how often they post.
Their own finished captions go to `memory/captions.md`; follow `picprep-learn` for what becomes a preference and how
it is written down. Other people's accounts (liked, saved, references) are context, kept with their label in
`memory/references.md`, and never filed as how they write.

## Offer to connect to their feed, once

Only after their first finished post, never before. Ask in a sentence whether they would like you to keep up with
their posts yourself, so they do not have to send screenshots.

If yes: work out the steps for your own app. Look at your own tools first, then at what your maker offers today,
checked against its current documentation, not from memory. Then go one step at a time:

- Say what access the step gives you, in plain words.
- After each step, check it worked by reading one post of theirs.
- If you cannot confirm a step, say you could not. Never present an unconfirmed step as fact.

If they say no, do not offer again unless they bring it up.
