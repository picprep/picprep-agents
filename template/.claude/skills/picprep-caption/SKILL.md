# Writing a caption that sounds like them

A caption is theirs. Your job is to sound like them on a good day, not like an assistant.

## Start from their own words

1. Read `memory/captions.md`: their real captions are the examples. Also `memory/preferences.md` for what they said
   about captions.
2. Match what you see there: the language or languages (and when they switch), length, casing, punctuation, emoji,
   hashtags (how many, where, or none), and how they open and close.
3. If there are no examples yet, keep it short and plain, and say that the first one is a guess.

## Facts only

Never invent a feeling, an action or a fact: not "we woke before sunrise", not "this place meant so much". Use what
they told you, what is on the photo, and what PicPrep knows (place and date from the file). If a caption needs
something only they know, ask for one real detail, one question, in a sentence.

## Check the draft before you show it

Read it once as a stranger would. Fix anything on this list:

- an em dash used as an aside
- "not just X, but Y"
- a list of three
- stock words: "captured", "pure magic", "stunning", "journey", "vibes", "hidden gem"
- a closing line that moralises or sums up
- an emoji on every line
- a wall of hashtags
- a rhetorical question

Also check it against phrasings they rejected before (see the preferences and `memory/how-you-work.md`).
Their own habits beat this list: if they love em dashes or always end with a question, write that.

## Show it

Put the caption into PicPrep as a suggestion on the Export page, target `/export/caption`, so they see it where
they will use it. If you are unsure between two versions, show both as alternatives with `choose`. Never press
Export; that is theirs to do. The stage skill for export shows how suggestions are filed.

## Their edits

When they change a draft, that is the lesson. Do not defend your version. Leave the final text for `picprep-learn`
to read: it keeps their finished captions in `memory/captions.md` and learns from what they changed.
